# Designing journey evidence maps

Journey maps become dangerous when polished diagrams blur research evidence with team imagination. Use this skill when mapping multi-stage user experiences across channels, time, actors, and system states.

## Decision ownership

Own journey scope, stages, evidence layers, confidence, channel/system events, actor boundaries, and opportunity linkage. Decide what can be represented as observed versus inferred or hypothesized.

## Inputs and evidence

Collect interviews, behavioral data, support contacts, analytics funnels, operational events, service blueprints, task observations, and known edge cases. Define population and scenario; one “universal journey” rarely fits all users.

## Procedure

Anchor stages to real user goals or transitions, not internal department boundaries. For each stage, separate observed actions, user-reported perceptions, system events, pain points, and interpretation. Attach provenance and confidence. Show alternate branches, loops, and failure paths when they materially affect the journey.

Overlay opportunities only after the evidence layer is clear. Avoid assigning emotions numerically without a defensible method. Include backstage/system constraints only when they explain user-visible outcomes.

## Failure topology

A linear happy-path map hides recovery and abandonment. Persona-style emotion curves can be invented decoration. Another failure is mixing several populations so contradictory paths appear as one incoherent journey.

Opportunities may be treated as validated needs merely because they are drawn beside evidence.

## Falsification

Trace every major claim to a source. Ask users or domain operators to challenge stage order and missing branches. Compare map transitions to funnel/operational data. Remove opportunity labels and verify the evidence layer remains independently useful.

## Output contract

Produce a `journey-evidence-maps-contract` defining scope/population, stages, evidence layers, provenance/confidence, alternate paths, system events, opportunity separation, and validation steps.
