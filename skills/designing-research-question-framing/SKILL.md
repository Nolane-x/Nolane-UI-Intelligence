# Designing research question framing

A weak research question produces expensive evidence that cannot change a decision. Use this skill before choosing methods when teams need to understand behavior, needs, comprehension, task success, adoption, or design risk.

## Decision ownership

Own the transformation from product uncertainty into answerable research questions, scope, target population, decision linkage, and falsifiable assumptions. Decide which questions require behavioral evidence versus attitudinal reports or product telemetry.

## Inputs and evidence

Collect the pending decision, competing options, assumptions, known evidence, product constraints, target users, expected consequences, and what the team would do differently depending on the result. Identify questions framed as “Do users like our new design?” or “Can we validate this?”

## Procedure

Start from the decision and uncertainty. Write questions about user behavior, comprehension, needs, constraints, or outcomes without embedding the preferred solution. Separate discovery questions from evaluative ones. Define population and context sufficiently to make answers actionable.

For each question, state what evidence would support, challenge, or leave the decision unresolved. Remove questions whose answers would not alter any planned action. Break broad topics into a prioritized set that a study can actually address.

## Failure topology

Leading questions generate confirmation. Questions about preference can substitute for task performance. Another failure is mixing several populations and contexts into one vague “users” category, making contradictory findings uninterpretable.

Teams may also ask a research question that analytics or technical inspection could answer more directly.

## Falsification

Ask stakeholders what decision changes for each possible answer. If nothing changes, reframe or remove the question. Have a researcher unfamiliar with the proposal infer the desired outcome; if it is obvious from wording, reduce bias. Map each question to a plausible method and evidence form.

## Output contract

Produce a `research-question-framing-contract` containing decision, uncertainty, prioritized neutral questions, population/context, evidence that would support/challenge each, method implications, and explicit out-of-scope questions.

## Handoffs

Use `designing-usability-test-protocols` for evaluative behavior, `designing-task-success-measures` for outcomes, interview research for generative questions, and `designing-design-hypothesis-ledgers` to track assumptions over time.
