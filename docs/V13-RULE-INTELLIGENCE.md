# NUI V13 Rule Intelligence

V13 turns UI rules into evidence-bounded operational contracts rather than a count-driven style blacklist.

## Authority boundary

- Reality/mechanical/behavioral/contextual rules may block only when their class, severity, applicability, observables and required evidence support that authority.
- Advisory, aesthetic and convergence signals never hard-block by themselves.
- A familiar font, gradient, centered hero, card grid or other isolated visual tell is not authorship proof.
- Missing required capability evidence remains UNKNOWN/BLOCKED rather than being converted into PASS.
- V10 empirical claim promotion remains bounded at `STRUCTURAL_ONLY` in repository-only CI.

## Rule contract

Canonical V13 rules carry explicit intent, applicability and non-applicability, failure modes, user impact, observables, falsifiers, repairs, exceptions, verification, owner/verifier hints, capability requirements, provenance IDs and lifecycle status.

## Provenance

Provenance records preserve what source or canonical owner supports a mechanism, who reported or maintains it, contraindications, and the transfer boundary. Source wording is not copied into canonical rule prose and source frequency does not become normative authority.

## Anti-duplication

The deterministic similarity court rejects exact operational duplicates, repeated failure/repair/verification signatures, component-noun substitution clones and catalog-wide boilerplate concentration while allowing legitimate near-neighbor rules whose failure, repair or verification mechanism differs.

## Composition

The current V13 catalog preserves all 75 V12 Reality Rules through a compatibility bridge and adds independently authored V13 foundation and first-wave rules. Rule count is reported as an inventory metric, never a quality quota.

## Current-head validation

`validate_repository()` composes inherited V7-V10 structural validation with V11 Runtime Doctor, V12 Reality/External UI integrity and V13 catalog/provenance/similarity checks. MCP and Python APIs expose bounded read-only inspection of the same current-head rule state.
